Chores Around the World

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Do children in other countries do the same chores as you? Here are some interesting facts. In Japan, students clean their own schools. Every day after lunch they sweep the floors, clean the windows and even wash the toilets. There are almost no cleaners in Japanese schools. Japanese people believe that this teaches respect and teamwork. In Kenya, many children in villages help to fetch water. Sometimes the river or the well is far from home, so they carry heavy containers for kilometres. In Sweden, parents and children often share chores equally. Boys and girls do the same jobs, and fathers cook as often as mothers. In Mexico, children often help their grandparents. Big families live together, and teenagers look after younger cousins. In Ukraine, many children help their grandparents in the garden in summer. They pick strawberries, water the vegetables and feed the chickens. Chores are different, but the idea is the same everywhere: a home is a team, and every member helps.
