Table Manners Around the World

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Imagine you are having dinner in another country. Do you know how to behave? Table manners are very different around the world, and what is polite in one place can be rude in another. In China and Japan people eat with chopsticks. Never stick your chopsticks straight up in a bowl of rice: people consider it very bad luck. In Bangladesh and in parts of India many people eat with their hands, but only with the right hand. The left hand is not used for food. In Britain people expect you to keep your hands on the table, not on your lap, and to say "please" and "thank you" all the time. In Korea the oldest person at the table starts eating first. Young people wait to show respect. And what about technology? In many families today, phones and other devices are not allowed at the table. "Dinner is for talking," says a mum from Kharkiv. "No item of technology can be more important than family." In Ukraine the host always serves too much food, and guests should try everything. If you leave an empty plate, you'll probably get more!
