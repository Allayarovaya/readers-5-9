Lesson 1

The Star Over Bethlehem

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Christmas is one of the most important holidays in many countries. It celebrates the birth of Jesus in Bethlehem more than two thousand years ago. But every nation has its own way to celebrate it. In Ukraine Christmas Eve is a family evening. People wait for the first star in the sky, and then the family sits down to a supper of twelve dishes. The most important dish is kutia, made of wheat, honey, poppy seeds and nuts. The day after, children go carolling. They sing old songs called koliadky and receive sweets and money. Many Ukrainian traditions have a link to the land and to the year of a farmer. The didukh, a sheaf of wheat in the corner of the room, represents the spirits of the family and a good harvest. Some families go to a midnight mass. Others decorate their homes with embroidered towels with old patterns. Every region has its own pattern, and some families believe that each pattern belongs to their own family. What do all these traditions have in common? Goodness, family and hope. They define who we are. Even people who are not religious relate to these traditions, because they connect us with our grandparents.
