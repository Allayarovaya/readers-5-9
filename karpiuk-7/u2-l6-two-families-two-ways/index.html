Lesson 6

Two Families, Two Ways

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Sofiia from Lutsk and Harry from Manchester are pen friends. Last month they compared housework in their families. In Sofiia's family everybody has fixed duties. Her mum cooks, her dad does the repairs, and Sofiia and her brother clean their rooms and wash up in turns. On Sundays the whole family cleans the flat together, and then they have a big family lunch. "My grandma visits every week and checks my room," Sofiia writes. "If it's messy, I hear about it for an hour!" Harry's family works differently. Every Sunday evening they write a list of jobs for the week on a whiteboard in the kitchen. Then everybody chooses jobs. Harry usually chooses cooking, because he loves making curry. His younger sister prefers looking after the garden and the rabbit. "We don't have fixed duties," Harry writes, "but if somebody forgets a job, they must do an extra one next week." Both friends agree on one thing: when everybody helps, the house is nicer and parents are less tired. And both of them hate cleaning the bathroom!
