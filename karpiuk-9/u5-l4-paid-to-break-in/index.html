Lesson 4

Paid to Break In

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
The Jobs Forum is held in our city every spring. Companies advertise their vacancies, universities give out leaflets, and school students have the opportunity to talk to people of fifty different professions. This year the longest queue was not for the pilot or the surgeon. It was for a quiet young woman in a grey hoodie with a sign: "Ethical hacker. Ask me anything." Her name was Zoriana, and she was twenty-six. "A hacker? Isn't that a crime?" was the first question. "It is, if you do it without permission," she said. "I am paid to do it. A bank, a shop or a hospital asks our company to attack its security system. I look for holes in it, just like a real criminal would. The difference is what happens next. A criminal steals. I write a report, and the hole is closed before anybody can use it." Who are her customers? "Any organisation that keeps information about people. Last month I tested an online shop. In two hours I could see the addresses of forty thousand customers. The owner turned pale. The next day it was fixed." She explained that hackers rarely "break" computers. They break habits. "The weakest part of any system is an individual who uses the password 12345 or clicks on a strange link. Once I got into an office building with a box of pizza. I said that I was delivering lunch, and somebody opened the door for me." Is it an attractive job? Zoriana counted on her fingers. It is well-paid. You can work from any place with the internet. And it is never boring, because you have to stay updated: new tricks appear every week, and a hacker who stops learning is useless in a year. "But there is pressure too," she added. "People trust you with their secrets. You sign serious documents. One wrong step, and you are not ethical any more." A boy asked how to start. "Don't try out your skills on your school's website," she laughed. "That is illegal. There are special legal competitions for teenagers where you can attack training systems. Learn Maths, English and programming. And learn to explain difficult things in simple words. Half of my job is writing reports that a director can understand." By the end of the day her leaflets had run out. So had the pizza jokes.
