Lesson 4

What the Guest Said and What the Paper Wrote

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Last month a well-known children's writer, Mr Andrii Lozynskyi, visited our school. Two reporters from the school newspaper were sent to his meeting with students. I was one of them. The other was Sviatoslav, a new member of our team. We sat in the same row and listened to the same answers. But our articles were very different. I recorded everything on my phone. In my article I wrote exactly what the guest had said. Mr Lozynskyi said that he wrote every morning from six to nine. He said that he had written his first story at the age of twelve and that it had been terrible. He told us that he was working on a new book about a girl detective. He said that he didn't like social media, because it took too much time. He added that he would come to our school again in spring. And he said that teenagers could become good writers if they read a lot. Sviatoslav didn't record anything. "I have a good memory," he told me. His article appeared on the school website the same evening under a huge headline: "Famous Writer Hates Teenagers and the Internet!" In his text Mr Lozynskyi said that he never used the internet. He said that modern teenagers couldn't write. He said that his new book was finished and that he was never coming back to our school. By the morning the article had two hundred angry comments. "Why did we invite him?" students wrote. Some of them wanted to throw his books out of the library. The editor called both of us. She played my recording and put the two texts side by side. Sviatoslav turned red. "I didn't want to lie," he said. "I thought that it would be more interesting. And I didn't remember the exact words." "A reporter is not a storyteller," said the editor. "When you write that a person said something, the person must have said it." The article was removed, and an apology was published. Sviatoslav wrote it himself. He also sent a letter to Mr Lozynskyi. The writer answered in two days. He said that he was not angry. He said that he had made the same mistake at his first job. And he told Sviatoslav that he was going to use this story in his next book.
