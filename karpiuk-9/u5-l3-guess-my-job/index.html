Reader Karpiuk 9 · Unit 5 · Jobs Around Us
Lesson 3

Guess My Job

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
At the Career Club our teacher asked five guests to describe their jobs without naming them. "Listen to the characteristics," she said, "and guess." Try it yourself. The answers are at the end of the text. Guest A. "My job is physically hard, and it can be dangerous. I work in shifts: twenty-four hours at work, then three days at home. Sometimes we wait for hours and nothing happens. Then the bell rings, and we have sixty seconds to get into the truck. I need to be strong, calm and quick. The job is not very well- paid, but when you carry a child out of the smoke, you don't think about money." Guest B. "I work indoors, and I sit a lot. My job needs patience and a good memory. I must be accurate, because one small mistake in a number can cost a company millions. Some people think that my work is boring. I find it relaxing. Numbers never lie, and at the end of the month everything must be in order." Guest C. "My job is creative and stressful at the same time. I work long hours, often at night, when the city is asleep. I am on my feet all day. My hands smell of garlic. A hundred people a day judge my work, and they do it in ten minutes. But it is rewarding to see empty plates." Guest D. "I work outdoors in any weather. My job is responsible: three hundred people trust me every day, although they never see my face. I have to concentrate for hours. I can't be late even for a minute, because the whole timetable depends on me. I see the sunrise more often than most people." Guest E. "My job is challenging, and no two days are the same. I need to be patient, fair and a good listener. I talk a lot, and I repeat the same thing in five different ways. I have long holidays, but I take my work home every evening. The results of my job can be seen only in ten or twenty years." The club members guessed four jobs out of five. Nobody guessed Guest B. "We thought you were a programmer," they said. The guest laughed. "Close. But I count money, not code." Answers: A is a firefighter, B is an accountant, C is a chef, D is a train driver, E is a teacher.
