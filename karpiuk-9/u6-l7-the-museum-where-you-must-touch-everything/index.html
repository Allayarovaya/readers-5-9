Lesson 7

The Museum Where You Must Touch Everything

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
In most museums the main rule is "Don't touch". Last Saturday our class visited a science museum where the rule is the opposite: "Please touch everything". There are no glass cases and no guards who whisper "Shh!". There are three floors of experiments, and you are the scientist. The first hall is about sound. I shouted into a long tube and heard my own voice come back two seconds later. My friend Illia stood between two huge metal plates and whispered a secret. Thirty metres away I heard every word. He was not happy about it. In the second hall we met electricity. A guide asked for a volunteer, and of course Illia raised his hand. He put it on a silver ball, and his hair stood up like the needles of a hedgehog. Everybody took photos. The guide explained that it was static electricity, the same force that makes a jumper crackle in the dark. My favourite hall was the room of mirrors and light. In one mirror I was as thin as a pencil, in another as round as a ball. There was a dark room with a wall that "remembers" your shadow. You jump, the lamp flashes, you walk away, and your shadow stays on the wall, still jumping. On the top floor there is a laboratory. A young chemist in a white coat showed us how to make a small "volcano" from soda and vinegar and how to freeze a flower in a second. Then she let us look at a drop of pond water under a microscope. It was full of tiny creatures that were swimming, turning and eating each other. I will never drink from a pond. The last room was the quietest. There was only a heavy ball on a long wire, slowly moving from side to side. A sign explained that the ball always moves in the same direction, and it is the floor, together with the whole planet, that turns under it. I stood there for ten minutes and felt the Earth move. On the bus home nobody played on their phones. We were arguing about why the sky is blue. Our Physics teacher was smiling. I think the museum did her homework for her.
