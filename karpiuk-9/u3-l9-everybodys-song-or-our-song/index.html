Lesson 9

Everybody's Song or Our Song?

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My cousin Kateryna and I are the same age, we live in the same street, and we can't agree about anything that has a melody. Kateryna loves pop culture. She knows every hit from the top ten, watches the most popular series and follows the stars that are followed by millions. "Why is it bad to like what everybody likes?" she asks. "A song becomes a hit because it is good. Yesterday the whole bus was singing the same chorus. Thirty strangers, and for three minutes we were friends. That is the magic of pop." She has a point. Pop culture is made for everybody. It is easy to find, easy to understand and easy to share. You can talk about a famous film with a classmate, a neighbour or a person from another country. I belong to a different world. I listen to folk-rock bands that play in small clubs for fifty people. My favourite group has never been shown on TV. Their albums are recorded in a garage, and their T-shirts are printed by the drummer's sister. When I meet somebody who knows them, we become friends in a minute, because there are so few of us. "That is exactly your problem," says Kateryna. "You like them because nobody knows them. If they become famous, you will say that they have sold out." I hate to admit it, but she has a point again. Subcultures are sometimes proud of being small. We look down on "ordinary" listeners, and that is not fair. Our Music teacher explained it well. "Pop culture and subcultures need each other," she said. "New ideas are born in small groups: in garages, in skate parks, in tiny clubs. Jeans, rap, graffiti and trainers were all parts of some subculture once. Then they were noticed, copied and sold to everybody. What is odd today will be in every shop tomorrow." Last month Kateryna took me to a big pop concert. I expected to be bored, and I sang until I lost my voice. A week later I took her to my garage band. She stood near the wall for three songs. Then she started dancing. "They are awesome," she said on the way home. "Don't tell anybody." "Why not?" "I want to be one of the first fans."
