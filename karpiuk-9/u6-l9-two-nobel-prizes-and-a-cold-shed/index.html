Lesson 9

Two Nobel Prizes and a Cold Shed

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
When your printer doesn't work or your video freezes, you probably say that technology hates you. Before you throw the laptop out of the window, read about a scientist whose working conditions were much worse than yours. Maria Sklodowska was born in Warsaw in 1867. She was the youngest of five children in a family of teachers. Maria was a brilliant pupil, but in those days women in her country were not allowed to study at university. So she and her sister made a plan. First Maria worked as a governess for several years and paid for her sister's studies in Paris. Then they changed roles. In 1891, at the age of twenty-four, Maria arrived in Paris. She lived in a tiny cold room under the roof and sometimes fainted from hunger over her books. Three years later she had two degrees, in Physics and in Mathematics. In Paris she met Pierre Curie, a scientist who was as quiet and as serious as she was. They got married and started working together. Their laboratory was an old shed with a leaking roof. It was freezing in winter and boiling in summer. In this shed Maria spent four years mixing tons of rock in a huge pot with an iron stick that was almost as tall as herself. The results were worth it. The Curies discovered two new chemical elements. Maria called the first one polonium, in honour of her native country. The second was radium. She also invented the word "radioactivity". In 1903 Marie Curie, as she was called in France, received the Nobel Prize in Physics together with her husband and another scientist. She was the first woman who won it. In 1911 she got a second Nobel Prize, this time in Chemistry. She is still the only person who has received the prize in two different sciences. During the First World War she organised mobile X-ray stations in cars, which helped doctors to treat wounded soldiers near the front line. She learnt to drive in order to do it. Marie Curie died in 1934. Scientists of that time didn't know how dangerous radiation was. Her notebooks are still radioactive, and people who want to read them must wear special clothes. She once said that nothing in life should be feared. It should only be understood.
