Lesson 7

The Message That Came a Day Late

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
This is a story about one voice message and a very long Saturday. I am telling it so that you never make my mistake. On Friday evening my friend Bohdana sent me a voice message. I was watching a series, so I listened to it with one ear. I remembered only the general idea: a birthday surprise for our friend Ilona, a cafe and a cake. On Saturday morning I tried to listen again, but my phone had no battery, and the charger was at my aunt's. So I called our classmate Ostap from the home phone. "What did Bohdana say yesterday?" I asked. "She said that we were meeting at the cafe near the bridge that day," said Ostap. "She said she had ordered the cake the day before, and she asked me to bring the balloons." I decided that "that day" meant Saturday. I bought flowers, put on a smart shirt and went to the cafe near the bridge at three o'clock. Nobody was there. I waited for an hour. I ate two ice creams. The waiter asked if I was waiting for a girl, and I said that I was waiting for five people and a cake. At four I went home, angry and full of ice cream. I got the charger back in the evening and finally listened to the message myself. "Hi! Listen carefully," Bohdana's voice said. "We are meeting at the cafe near the bridge tomorrow at three. No, wait, not tomorrow. Tomorrow I am busy. The day after tomorrow, on Sunday! I ordered the cake yesterday. Tell Ostap to bring the balloons. And don't tell Ilona. See you there next time, I mean, this Sunday!" I sat down on the floor. She had said "tomorrow" and then changed it. Ostap had heard the message on Friday night, and when he repeated it to me, "tomorrow" became "that day" and "yesterday" became "the day before". He had reported it correctly. I had understood it wrongly. On Sunday I came to the cafe again. The waiter smiled. "Are the five people and the cake coming today?" he asked. They came. Ilona was surprised, the cake was delicious, and the flowers were only one day old. Since then I have had a rule. When somebody tells me a time, I ask one more question: "Which day exactly?"
