Lesson 6

Five Days in Toronto

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My aunt Svitlana has lived in Canada for twelve years. Last August she invited me to Toronto, the largest city in the country. I kept a travel diary, and here are the best pages. Day 1. The first thing I noticed was the lake. Lake Ontario is so big that you can't see the other side, and gulls fly over it like over the sea. The city has a long waterfront with parks, bike paths and wooden benches. We walked there in the evening, and I counted nine languages around me. My aunt says that half of the people in Toronto were born in another country. Day 2. The CN Tower. It is more than five hundred and fifty metres tall, and for over thirty years it was the tallest tower in the world. A lift with a glass floor took us to the observation deck in less than a minute. My stomach revolted, but the view was worth it: toy cars, toy boats and the lake as flat as a mirror. There is a place where you can stand on thick glass and look straight down. I did it. My aunt took a photo of my face. I will never show it to anybody. Day 3. The Royal Ontario Museum. I planned to spend an hour there and stayed for five. The exhibits include dinosaur skeletons, Egyptian mummies and a whole hall of crystals. My favourite exhibit was a small leather canoe made by the first peoples of Canada. It is three hundred years old and looks as if it could still float. Day 4. We followed my aunt's favourite route through the city by tram: Chinatown, Little Italy, a Greek street and a Ukrainian shop where I found varenyky and a newspaper in my own language. Toronto is like a world atlas that you can cross in one afternoon. Day 5. Niagara Falls, an hour and a half from the city. No photo can show it. You hear the water long before you see it. We took a boat that goes almost under the falls. Everybody gets a raincoat, and it doesn't help at all. On the way to the airport I asked my aunt what she missed most. "Cherries from Grandma's garden," she said. "And people who can pronounce my name." I promised to send her a recording of all of us saying "Svitlana".
