Lesson 7

Turn Left at the Lion

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
On the second day of our school trip to London my phone battery died, and I got separated from my group. I had no map. I had only the name of our meeting point: the British Museum, at four o'clock. It was half past two. For the first time in my life I had to ask real people for directions in real English. I was standing in Trafalgar Square, next to a fountain. A woman was feeding pigeons, although a sign said that it was forbidden. "Excuse me, could you tell me the way to the British Museum?" "Oh, it is not far, love. Go past the big column with the lions. Take the road on the right, opposite the church. Go straight on until you get to a crossroads with a theatre on the corner. Then ask again." I went past the lions, found the church and walked straight on. At the crossroads there were four theatres, one on each corner. I chose a man with a newspaper. "Excuse me, how do I get to the British Museum?" "On foot? Let me think. Cross the road at the traffic lights. Walk along this street as far as the bookshop. Turn left, then take the second turning on the right. You will see a small square with a garden in the middle. The museum is just behind it. You can't miss it." I could, and I did. I turned left at the wrong bookshop. London, it turned out, is full of bookshops. Ten minutes later I was in front of a pub called "The Red Lion", and there was no museum anywhere. A girl of my age was sitting on the steps with a skateboard. "Excuse me, I am lost. Is the British Museum near here?" She laughed. "You are quite close. It is at the end of this street, next to the park. Come on, I am going that way." Her name was Priya. On the way she asked where I was from and told me that her best friend's grandmother came from Ternopil. She left me at the gate, between two huge columns, at five to four. My teacher was counting heads. "Where have you been?" she asked. "Practising English," I said. And it was true. In an hour and a half I had said more English sentences than in a month of lessons. Sometimes the best thing a phone can do for you is to die.
