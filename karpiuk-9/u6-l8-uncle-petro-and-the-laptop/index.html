Lesson 8

Uncle Petro and the Laptop

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My uncle Petro is a brilliant carpenter. He can build a staircase without a single nail. But until last month he had never touched a computer. Then his customers started asking for a website, and he bought a laptop. I am his niece, I am fifteen, and I was chosen as his teacher. Here are my notes from our first lesson. 10.00. I tell him to plug in the laptop and turn it on. He looks for a handle. I show him the button. The screen lights up, and he says, "Not bad." 10.05. We have to set up his account. I ask him to key in a password. He types "password". I explain that it is the worst password in the world. He types "Petro". We agree on something longer. 10.15. He logs in. It takes three attempts, because he has already forgotten the password. I write it on a piece of paper and then tell him never to do that. 10.30. I teach him to click on an icon. He clicks so hard that the table shakes. "Gently," I say. "It is not a hammer." 10.45. We open a page with photos of furniture. I show him how to scroll down. He scrolls so fast that he reaches the bottom in a second. I show him how to scroll up. He is impressed. "Like a lift," he says. 11.00. He wants to print out a picture of a chair. The printer is not connected. We find the cable. The printer wakes up and prints out the chair eleven times, because he has clicked eleven times. 11.20. Suddenly the internet is cut off. My uncle is sure that he has broken it. In fact my aunt has unplugged the router to plug in the iron. 11.40. I tell him about people who hack into computers and steal information. He looks at the laptop as if it were a dangerous animal. I teach him to back up his files and to log out when he finishes. 12.00. Time to shut down. He presses the button and holds it. The screen goes black. "Was that right?" he asks. It was not, but I am too tired to explain. Yesterday my uncle sent me his first email. It had no text, only a photo of a new table. Under the table, on the floor, I could see eleven printed chairs.
