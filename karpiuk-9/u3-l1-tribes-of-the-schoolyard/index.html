Reader Karpiuk 9 · Unit 3 · We Are Young And Cool
Lesson 1

Tribes of the Schoolyard

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Stand in any schoolyard during the long break and look around. You will see that students don't mix like sugar in tea. They gather in small groups, and each group has its own clothes, music and jokes. Sociologists call such groups youth subcultures. Our reporter spent a week in the yard of School No. 12 and met four of them. The skaters meet on the steps behind the gym. They wear wide jeans and trainers with flat soles, and their knees are always covered with plasters. "Skating is not about tricks," says Luka, 15. "It is about falling and getting up again. When somebody learns a new jump, all of us cheer." The K-pop fans sit on the benches near the lime tree. They learn the dances of their favourite Korean bands from videos and practise them every break. "People think that we only scream at concerts," says Milana. "In fact, three girls from our group have started learning Korean, and one of us draws her own comics." The gamers don't go outside at all. They stay in the IT room and discuss levels, maps and strategies. Their slang is hard to understand, but their teamwork is perfect. "In a game you can't win alone," explains Rostyslav. "You learn to trust your team." The eco-activists are a new tribe. They carry reusable bottles, wear second-hand clothes and collect plastic caps for recycling. "We are not a fashion," says Varvara. "We just want the planet to last longer than our phones." Why do teenagers join such groups? Psychologists say that a subculture gives a young person three things: a feeling of belonging, a way to be different from parents and a chance to express who they are. It is a kind of rehearsal for adult life. Of course, there is a danger too. Some groups look down on everybody who is not like them. "That is the difference between a tribe and a cage," says the school psychologist. "A good group lets you in and lets you out." On Friday our reporter saw something interesting. Luka the skater was teaching Milana to stand on a board, and she was showing him a dance step. Both were laughing. Tribes are fine, but bridges are better.
