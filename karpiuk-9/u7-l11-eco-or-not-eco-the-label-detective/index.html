Eco or Not Eco? The Label Detective

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Green leaves on a bottle. The word "natural" in big letters. A picture of a happy dolphin. Does it mean that the product is good for the planet? Not always. Companies know that people want to buy "green" things, and some of them paint their goods green only on the outside. Our class decided to become label detectives. We went to a supermarket with notebooks and found four tricks. Trick 1. Beautiful words. "Eco-friendly", "natural", "green". These words sound nice, but no law explains what they mean. A washing powder called "Green Meadow" had a long list of strong chemicals on the back. If a product is really eco-friendly, it says exactly why. Trick 2. Pictures. A bottle of shower gel was decorated with a waterfall and a forest. The bottle itself was made of plastic that can't be recycled in our town. The forest was only on the label. Trick 3. One good thing. A pack of biscuits said "Now with a paper box!" in a green circle. Inside the paper box each biscuit was wrapped in its own plastic. One small improvement was used to hide a big problem. Trick 4. Proud of nothing. A spray had a sign: "Doesn't destroy the ozone layer!" Our Chemistry teacher laughed. The gas that destroyed it was banned many years ago, so no spray contains it. It is like writing "without dinosaurs" on a bottle of milk. How can you tell the truth from a trick? We made three rules. First, turn the product over and read the small letters on the back, not the big ones on the front. Second, look for official eco-labels: they are given by independent organisations after real tests. Third, ask the simplest question: "Do I need this thing at all?" The greenest product is often the one that you don't buy. Water was the last topic of our project. Everything that we pour into the sink travels to the river: washing powder, shampoo, oil from the frying pan. A filter station stops a lot, but not everything. So what we choose in the shop is also what the fish will swim in. Our poster "Read the Back!" now hangs near the school canteen. The supermarket manager asked for a copy. We are still deciding if it was a compliment.
