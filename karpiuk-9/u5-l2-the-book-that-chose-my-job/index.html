Lesson 2

The Book That Chose My Job

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Our school librarian, Ms Halyna, has an unusual shelf near her desk. It is called "Books That Changed Somebody's Life". Any visitor of the library can put a book there with a short note inside. Last month our class read the notes, and three of them started a long discussion. The first note was in a thick old volume about the stars. "I read this book when I was twelve, during a long illness," wrote Mr Taras, our Physics teacher. "I didn't understand half of it, but I couldn't stop. Since then I have never looked at the night sky in the same way. I intended to become an astronomer. I became a teacher instead, and I don't regret it. Every year I give this book to one curious student." The second note was hidden in a detective novel. "People say that detective stories are not serious literature," wrote a graduate of our school. "This one taught me to notice small details and not to believe the first explanation. Today I work as an investigator. My job is less exciting than in novels, with more paper and fewer car chases, but the main rule is the same: facts first." The third note surprised us most. It was in a cookbook with spots of oil on the pages. "I was a bad student," wrote the author. "I hated reading. Then my grandmother gave me this book and said, 'If you can read a recipe, you can cook a dinner. If you can cook a dinner, nobody will call you useless.' I cooked every recipe from page 1 to page 214. Now I am a chef, and I own a small restaurant. It was the first book that I finished, and it proved that I could finish things." After reading the notes we argued for the whole lesson. Can a book really choose a job for you? Marko said that it was nonsense: you choose a profession because of money and qualifications. Olesia disagreed. "A book can't give you a degree," she said. "But it can show you a door. Without that cookbook the boy would never have entered a kitchen." Ms Halyna listened and smiled. "Bring your own books in ten years," she said. "There is still some room on the shelf."
