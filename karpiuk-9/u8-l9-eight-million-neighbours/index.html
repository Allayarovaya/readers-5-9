Lesson 9

Eight Million Neighbours

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My cousin Nazar and I grew up in the same village of nine hundred people. Three years ago his family moved to London, where his father had found work. This spring I visited him for a week. I wanted to know one thing: what is it like to live in a city of more than eight million? "Loud," said Nazar. "That was my first impression. In our village you can hear a dog on the other side of the river. Here you can't hear your own thoughts. There are sirens, buses and planes all day long. It took me half a year to learn to sleep." His day starts at half past six. The journey to school takes fifty minutes: a bus, then the underground, where people stand so close that nobody needs to hold on. "At home I walked to school in seven minutes," he says. "But there I had one school to choose from. Here I had twenty." That is the main thing about a big city, Nazar explains: choice. He can learn robotics, Japanese or fencing. There are free museums, huge libraries and concerts every night. In his class there are children from fourteen countries, and at lunch he can try food that he didn't know existed. "I have friends from Nigeria, Poland and India. In the village everybody was somebody's cousin." But a big city has its price. Everything is expensive: his parents pay more for a small flat than a house costs in our village. Nobody knows the neighbours. "We have lived on this floor for three years," says Nazar, "and I know the name of only one person next door. It is the cat. His name is Winston." The air is not as fresh, and to see a real forest you have to travel for an hour. On my last evening we climbed a hill in a park. The whole city was below us, a sea of lights from one end of the sky to the other. "Would you come back?" I asked. Nazar thought for a long time. "If I could take the museums and my friends with me, I would," he said. "I miss the silence and Grandma's garden. But I am not the same person as three years ago. A big city makes you fast, brave and a little lonely. I am still deciding if it is a good exchange." Below us eight million windows were shining. Somewhere among them Winston was waiting for his dinner.
