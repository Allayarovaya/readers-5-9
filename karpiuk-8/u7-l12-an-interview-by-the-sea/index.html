Lesson 12

An Interview by the Sea

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
This is part of a radio programme about tourism. The journalist is talking to Mr Oleh, who has worked as a guide on the Black Sea coast for twenty years. Journalist: Mr Oleh, how long is Ukraine's coastline? Guide: It is almost three thousand kilometres, if you count the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov together. There are sandy beaches, rocks, river mouths and salt lakes. Journalist: What makes the Black Sea coast special? Guide: The combination of sea, steppe and history. In one day you can swim, see pink flamingos on a lake and walk among the ruins of an ancient Greek city. Journalist: Which resorts were the most popular before the war? Guide: Odesa, of course, with its beaches and its famous humour. Zatoka, with its long sand line. Small quiet villages for families. And Crimea, which is Ukraine and which we are waiting for. Journalist: What is the weather like in the season? Guide: The season lasts from June to September. The water is warmest in August, about twenty-four degrees. It rarely rains, so bring a hat. Journalist: What should a tourist try? Guide: Fresh fish, watermelons from Kherson and grapes. And sunrise over the sea. It is free. Journalist: The war has changed everything on the coast. What do you dream about? Guide: I dream of the day when the beaches are safe and full of children again. The sea is patient. It will wait for us. Journalist: So what is your conclusion? Guide: My conclusion is simple. A coast is not only sand and water. It is the people who love it. And we haven't gone anywhere.
