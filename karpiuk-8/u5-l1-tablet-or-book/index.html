Reader Karpiuk 8 · Unit 5 · Do You Need A Book?
Lesson 1

Tablet or Book?

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My sister Ruslana and I share a room, and we argue about one thing every evening. She reads paper books. I read on a tablet. Each of us is sure that the other one is wrong. "A tablet is better," I say. "I have two hundred books in one small thing. It is light. I can read in the dark without a lamp. And if I don't know a word, I touch it and see the translation." "A book is better," says Ruslana. "It doesn't need a charger. It smells nice. I can see how many pages are left. And nobody will steal a book on a beach." "Paper books are expensive, and they take a lot of space. Look at your shelves!" "I can borrow them from the library for free. And your eyes get tired from the screen. You have told me that yourself." That is true. Generally, I read faster on a tablet, but I remember less. With a tablet it is also easy to "just check one message". Then an hour disappears. Last week our Ukrainian teacher asked the class, "Who is in favour of paper books?" Fourteen hands. "Who is in favour of tablets?" Twelve hands. Nobody won. "It is not so complicated," said the teacher. "A book is a text and a reader. The rest is packaging. Read the way you like, but read." In the evening the electricity went off for three hours. My tablet had five per cent of battery. Ruslana lit a candle and opened her book. Then she moved it a little, so that I could see the page too. We read together. I didn't say anything about tablets.
