Lesson 1

Every School Is Special

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My cousin Denys and I are both fourteen, but our schools are completely different. Last weekend we compared them. I go to an ordinary state school in a small town. It is a large grey building with six hundred students. There are thirty people in my class. We don't wear a uniform. Our teachers are different: some are strict, and some are very tolerant. Our Maths teacher never shouts, even when we deserve it. Denys studies at a private school in Lviv. There are only fourteen students in his class. They wear dark blue jackets with a small badge. Good manners are very important there. Students stand up when a teacher comes in, and they are not allowed to use phones even during the breaks. "It is the norm for us," says Denys. "At first it was hard. Now I don't even think about my phone until four o'clock." We tried to analyse which school is better. His school has a swimming pool and a robotics club. Mine has a huge old garden, a football team that wins everything and a cook who makes the best pancakes in the region. His teachers know every student very well. My school has taught me to find friends among very different people. "I think that a school is special not because of the building," said Denys. "It is special because of the people." I agreed. Then he added, "But I would still like to try your pancakes." So next Friday he is coming to my school for lunch. Our cook has already been warned.
