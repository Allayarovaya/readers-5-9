Do You Need a Book?

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
At the end of the unit our teacher asked the question from its title: "Do you need a book?" She didn't want an essay. She wanted one true story from each of us. Here are four. Artem: "Last summer I broke my leg and spent three weeks in bed. For the first two days I played games on my phone. Then I got bored. My aunt brought me a novel about pirates, which had six hundred pages. I read it in a week. I didn't notice that my leg hurt. So yes, I needed a book." Viktoriia: "I don't read much fiction. But when I wanted to learn to bake, I consulted three cookbooks from the library. Videos are too fast. A book waits for you." Sashko: "My little brother couldn't fall asleep without me, and I was angry about it. Then I started reading fairy tales to him. Now it is the best part of my day. He knows the ending of every story, but he still asks, 'And then?'" Emiliia: "I moved here from another city, and I had no friends. In the library I saw a girl with the same fantasy book that I was reading. I asked, 'Which part are you on?' We have been friends for a year." The teacher wrote four words on the board: a doctor, a teacher, a bridge, a friend. "That is what a book can be," she said. "Generally, people who say that they don't need books just haven't met the right one." Then she asked, "And which book is waiting for you now?"
