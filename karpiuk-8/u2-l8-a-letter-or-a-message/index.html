Lesson 8

A Letter or a Message?

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Our class had a discussion: "Modern or traditional communication: which is better?" The teacher divided us into two teams. I was in the "traditional" team, although I had sent forty messages that morning. The "modern" team spoke first. "A message is fast," said Illia. "I can write to my dad at work and get an answer in a minute. A letter takes a week." "Video calls are amazing," said Marta. "My sister studies in Poland. We see each other every evening. A hundred years ago we would see each other once a year." "And it is cheap. Stamps and envelopes cost money. A message is free." Then it was our turn. "A message is fast, but people forget it fast too," I said. "My grandmother has kept all the letters from my grandfather. They are fifty years old. Will you keep your messages for fifty years?" "A letter has a smell and handwriting," said Vika. "You can see that the person was in a hurry or that a tear fell on the paper." "In a real conversation you see a face," said Andrii. "An emoji can lie. Eyes can't." The "modern" team answered, "But we can send photos, songs and voice messages. Isn't a voice real?" The discussion lasted the whole lesson. Nobody won. "Both are good," said the teacher. "The tool is not important. What you say is important." For homework we had to write a real paper letter to somebody. I wrote to my grandmother. She called me in tears. Then she sent me a message with a heart. She has learned how.
