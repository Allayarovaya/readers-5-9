Lesson 9

Where the Roads Meet

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Why is the geographical position of a country so important? Because it decides who your neighbours are, what you can grow and how you can trade. Ukraine's position is special in several ways. First, the latitude. Ukraine lies between about forty-four and fifty-two degrees north. It is not too cold and not too hot. Together with fertile soil, this makes the country perfect for agriculture. Second, the seas. Ukraine has a long coast on the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov. The seas are warm, and the ports can work for most of the year. Ships carry Ukrainian grain to Africa, Asia and Europe. Third, the rivers. The Dnipro crosses the country from north to south and is navigable for a long distance. In the south-west Ukraine reaches the Danube, the second longest river in Europe. The Danube flows through ten countries, so it is a water road to the centre of the continent. The Carpathians are an important watershed. Most rivers that start there flow to the Black Sea, but some flow north to the Baltic Sea. Fourth, the land roads. Ukraine lies between Western Europe and Asia. Railways, motorways and pipelines cross its territory. The shortest way from Central Europe to Transcaucasia and Central Asia often goes through Ukraine or across the Black Sea. Trade has followed these roads for more than a thousand years. A position like this is a great opportunity. It is also a great responsibility. A country at a crossroads needs to be strong, because everybody wants to use the road.
