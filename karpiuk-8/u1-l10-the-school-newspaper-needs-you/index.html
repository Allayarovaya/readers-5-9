Lesson 10

The School Newspaper Needs You

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Our school newspaper is called "The Bell". It comes out once a month, and I am the editor this year. Last week we had a meeting to plan the September issue. It was not easy. "We need a main article," I said. "Any ideas?" "Let's write about the new uniform," said Vira. "Everybody is talking about the stripes. Half of the school loves them, and half hates them." "Good. Take photos and ask ten students for their opinions. And please ask the teachers too." "I want to write about freedom," said Ostap. He is our philosopher. "Do students have enough freedom at school? Can we choose anything?" "Interesting, but it must be short. Last time your article was three pages long, and nobody finished it." Nazar offered a page about good manners. "A funny one. 'Ten things you should never do in the canteen.' With drawings." "Yes! That is exactly what we need." Yana wanted to interview the oldest person in the neighbourhood, a woman of ninety-two who studied at our school before the war. "She remembers when there was one classroom for four forms and children wrote with ink." Everybody was silent for a moment. "That is the main article," I said. "Not the uniform. Sorry, Vira." Vira wasn't upset. "Then I will take photos for Yana." We finished at five. We had an old woman's story, a funny page, a short article about freedom and an argument about stripes. The issue came out on Friday. By Monday there wasn't a single copy left. Even the cleaner asked for one.
