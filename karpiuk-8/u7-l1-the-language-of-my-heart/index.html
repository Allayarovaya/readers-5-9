Lesson 1

The Language of My Heart

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Last year my class took part in a competition of essays called "My Country, My Language". This is the essay that won. It was written by Zlata, who is fourteen. "When I was little, I thought that a language was just words. Now I know that it is much more. A language is the identity of a nation. It keeps its songs, its jokes and its memory. The Ukrainian language is soulful and melodic. People call it the nightingale's language. It has gentle words for everything: for a mother, for the sun, even for a small river. The history of our language is a history of struggle. For hundreds of years invaders tried to forbid it. Books were burned, schools were closed, and people were tempted to forget who they were. But the language survived, because mothers sang lullabies in it and poets wrote in it. You can't kill a song. My country is like its language. It is picturesque: golden fields of wheat under a blue sky, green mountains, old towns. It is industrial too, with big factories and modern cities. Its people are hospitable. A guest in a Ukrainian home never leaves hungry. Kyiv, with its golden domes above the Dnipro, is often called the pearl of our land. Ukrainians have always valued freedom and dignity more than a quiet life. We know that evil is real, and we know that it can be stopped. Today, when my country has to struggle for its freedom again, I understand the words of our anthem better than ever. I speak Ukrainian not because I have to. I speak it because it is the language of my heart."
