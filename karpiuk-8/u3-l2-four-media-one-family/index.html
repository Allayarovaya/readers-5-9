Reader Karpiuk 8 · Unit 3 · Do We Need The Mass Media?
Lesson 2

Four Media, One Family

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
In our family nobody gets news in the same way. I noticed it last Sunday at breakfast. Grandpa was reading a newspaper. A real paper one. He buys it every morning at the kiosk near the bus stop. "I like the smell," he says. "And nobody can switch it off." He reads everything, from the first page to the crossword. Grandma had the radio on in the kitchen. She listens while she cooks. She knows the names of all the presenters and argues with them aloud. They never answer. Dad was watching the news on TV with his coffee. He prefers television, because he wants to see things with his own eyes. He watches the evening news too, and then he tells us what we have already read online. Mum was looking at her tablet. She reads news websites and compares three different sources. "One source is an opinion," she says. "Three sources are information." And me? I was scrolling social media on my phone. I learned about a football result, a new song and a cat that can open doors. Mum asked, "And what happened in the country today?" I wasn't sure. "The mass media are like food," said Grandpa. "You can eat only sweets, but you will be hungry and ill." So we made a deal. Every Sunday each of us tells one important piece of news and says where it came from. Last week Grandma's radio won. She knew about the new bridge before the internet did. I still love the cat videos. But now I read a real article first.
