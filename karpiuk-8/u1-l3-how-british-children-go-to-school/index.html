Lesson 3

How British Children Go to School

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My penfriend Alice lives in Manchester. Last month she explained to me how education in Britain works. It is quite different from ours. Children start primary school at the age of five. Primary education lasts for six years. At eleven they move to a secondary school. Secondary education is compulsory until the age of sixteen. Most children go to a comprehensive school. It is free and takes all children from the neighbourhood. Alice goes to a school like this. There are also grammar schools. To get a place there, children must pass a difficult exam at eleven. Some parents choose a boarding school, where students live and study. They go home only for holidays. Many of these schools are private and very expensive. And some children don't go to school at all. Their parents homeschool them, sometimes with the help of a tutor. A typical day starts at about nine with an assembly. The whole school meets in the hall. The head teacher gives the news and sometimes awards. Afterwards students go to their lessons. School finishes at about half past three. Almost all students wear a uniform. Alice hates her tie, but she likes not thinking about clothes in the morning. At sixteen students take important exams in eight or nine subjects. Afterwards they can leave school or stay for two more years to prepare for university. "And one more difference," wrote Alice. "We start in September too, but nobody brings flowers to the teachers. That is a nice tradition. Can I borrow it?"
