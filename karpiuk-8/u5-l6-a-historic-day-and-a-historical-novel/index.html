Lesson 6

A Historic Day and a Historical Novel

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
English has two words that look almost the same: "historic" and "historical". Even native speakers mix them up. Our teacher explained the difference with a story about one Saturday. Last Saturday our class went on an excursion to an old town. It was a town with a long history. In the morning we visited a historical museum. It is a museum about history. We saw old coins, weapons and maps. The guide was wearing a historical costume: a long dress like the ones women wore three hundred years ago. Then we walked to the castle. It is a historic building, which means that it is important in history. Kings and hetmans stayed there. In the main hall there was a historic meeting in the seventeenth century, when an important agreement was signed. Outside the town we saw the field of a historic battle. Today it is just a quiet field with a small monument, but it is a historic place. On the bus home I was reading a historical novel. It is fiction, but the story takes place in the past. In the evening my parents watched a historical drama on TV, a film about real events of the past. "So," said the teacher, "what is the rule?" "Historical means 'about history' or 'connected with the past'," said Lida. "A novel, a film, a museum, a costume." "And historic means 'very important in history'. A day, a place, a battle, a meeting." "Correct. And today is a historic day," said the teacher, "because the whole class has understood the rule the first time."
