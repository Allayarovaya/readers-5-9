Lesson 13

A Day in the Life of a Young Champion

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
People often think that the lives of sports stars are easy: medals, cameras and holidays by the sea. Our school newspaper interviewed Yaryna, a seventeen-year-old swimmer from our town who is a member of the national youth team. Her day looks different. "I get up at 5.15," she says. "At six I am already in the water. The morning training lasts two hours. I swim about five kilometres before most people have breakfast." At 8.30 she goes to school. "I am used to doing homework on buses and in changing rooms. My teachers help me, but nobody gives me marks for medals." After lessons she has lunch and a short rest. Her diet is strict. "I don't follow a special diet, but I eat simple food: porridge, fish, vegetables, fruit. I used to love chips. Now I eat them twice a year, on my birthday and after the main competition." At four there is the second training: the gym and the pool again. She comes home at seven. "I go to bed at 9.30. I don't go to parties. I have missed a lot of birthdays. My friends used to be upset, but now they come to my competitions with posters." Is she happy? "Yes. I have chosen this life myself. If I win, I am happy. If I lose, I learn something. The hardest thing is not the training. It is getting up when it is dark and cold and nobody is watching." Her dream is the Olympic Games. Her advice is short: "Don't look for an easy life. Look for the life that is yours."
