Lesson 3

Tabloid or Quality Paper?

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Last month our class visited the office of a local newspaper. The editor, Mrs Olena, met us in a room full of old newspapers. "The press is older than you think," she said. "The first printed newspapers appeared in Europe more than four hundred years ago. And they are still alive." She explained that periodicals are different. A daily newspaper comes out every day. A weekly comes out once a week, and a monthly magazine once a month. Her newspaper is a weekly. It has a print edition and an online edition. Then she put two papers on the table. "This one is a quality newspaper. It writes about politics, the economy and culture. It checks every fact and names its sources. And this one is a tabloid. Big photos, big headlines, stories about stars. It is easy to read, but it is not always true." "How can we tell the difference?" asked Pavlo. "Look at three things. Does the article say where the data came from? Does it give two sides of a story? Does the headline shout?" She showed us how an issue is made. On Monday journalists choose topics. By Wednesday the articles must be ready. On Thursday the pages are designed, and at night the paper is printed. "Our job is to inform people, not to frighten them," she said. "Many readers use a tablet now instead of paper. That is fine. The format changes. The rules of honest journalism don't." At the end she gave each of us a fresh copy. It was still warm. On page five there was a photo of our class.
