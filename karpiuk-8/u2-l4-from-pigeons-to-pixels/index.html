Lesson 4

From Pigeons to Pixels

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
How did people send messages two thousand years ago? They used runners, fire signals and even pigeons. A letter could travel for months. Communication was slow, expensive and not very reliable. The development of communication is a long process. In the fifteenth century the printing press made books cheaper. In the nineteenth century the telegraph could transfer a short message across the ocean in minutes. Then came the telephone, the radio and television. Each invention made the world a little smaller. The biggest change happened at the end of the twentieth century, when the internet became a worldwide network. Today billions of people are connected. We can download a book in seconds. We can see a friend on the other side of the planet. A smartphone functions as a telephone, a camera, a map, a library and a cinema at the same time. Have modern means of communication replaced the old ones? Not completely. People still write letters and postcards, though much less often. We still listen to the radio in the car. Paper books are still sold in millions. And one thing has not changed at all. No technology can replace an ordinary talk with a friend who is sitting next to you. A virtual hug is not a real hug. Scientists say that we send more messages than ever before, but many people feel lonely. So perhaps the question is not how fast we can communicate. The question is whether we have something to say and somebody to listen.
