A Letter from an American School

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Hi everybody! My name is Tyler. I am fourteen, and I live in Ohio, in the USA. Your teacher asked me to tell you about American schools. Our system has three steps. We start elementary school at five or six. At eleven we go to middle school, which is also called junior high. At fourteen we start high school and stay there for four years. I have just started, so I am a "freshman". Most kids go to public schools. They are free. I take a yellow school bus every morning, just like in the movies. Some kids go to private schools, and some are homeschooled by their parents. We don't wear a uniform. We don't stay in one classroom either. After every lesson we go to our lockers, change books and run to the next room. We have five minutes for that. We choose some of our subjects. This year I have chosen Spanish and Photography. Sport is a big thing here. Our school has teams for football, basketball and baseball. On Friday nights the whole town comes to watch the football game. The cheerleading team dances and shouts, and the school band plays. The rules are strict about one thing: cheating. If you copy somebody's work in a test, you get a zero, and your parents get a phone call. What else? Lunch is in a huge cafeteria, and pizza day is everybody's favourite. What is your school like? Do you really stay with the same classmates for all eleven years? That sounds amazing! Write back! Tyler
