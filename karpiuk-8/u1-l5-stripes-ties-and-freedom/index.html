Lesson 5

Stripes, Ties and Freedom

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Our school is going to introduce a uniform next year. The head teacher asked students for their opinions. Here are four letters from the school newspaper. Zoriana, 14: "I am against it. Clothes show who you are. A uniform takes away our freedom. In my old school we had jackets with green and yellow stripes. We looked like tired bees." Arsenii, 15: "I am for it. In the morning I waste twenty minutes choosing what to wear. With a uniform everybody looks the same, and nobody laughs at you because your trainers are not fashionable. That is a kind of freedom too." Milana, 13: "It depends on the uniform. A grey skirt and an old-fashioned jacket? No, thank you. But a comfortable hoodie with the school badge? Why not? Let students choose the design." Tymofii, 14: "My cousin studies in England. If his tie is not on, he gets detention. He has to stay after lessons for an hour. Once he got detention for odd socks: one black and one blue! I think that is too strict. A uniform is fine, but the rules must be reasonable." The head teacher read all the letters and organised a vote. The result: sixty per cent for a uniform, but only if students design it themselves. The competition for the best design starts on Monday. Zoriana is taking part. "If we must have a uniform," she says, "I want to make sure that nobody looks like a bee again."
