Lesson 12

The World Fair in Room 14

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
For our final project we turned our classroom into a world fair. There were five tables, one for each country. Parents and younger students were invited as visitors. Each visitor got a "passport" and had to collect five stamps. The British table was the most polite. Visitors were offered tea with milk and had to stand in a queue. Ivanna, in a paper crown, taught everybody to say "Lovely weather, isn't it?" even though it was raining. At the American table there was a map with fifty states. Visitors had to find three of them in one minute. Nobody could find Ohio. Denys explained what a melting pot is with a real pot and five kinds of sweets. The Canadian table had a red maple leaf made of real autumn leaves. Visitors had to say "sorry" in two languages: English and French. There was also a photo of the giant pysanka, and everybody was surprised. At the Australian table Maksym played a cardboard didgeridoo. It sounded like a sick cow, but he was very proud. Visitors painted animals with dots. The New Zealand table was the loudest. Our boys had learned a short haka. Every ten minutes the floor shook. The little kids screamed with joy and asked for more. I was at the door with a stamp for the passports. My job was to ask one question: "What have you learned today?" A boy from the second form thought hard and said, "That English is not one country. It is a lot of countries. And I want to see them all." I gave him two stamps.
