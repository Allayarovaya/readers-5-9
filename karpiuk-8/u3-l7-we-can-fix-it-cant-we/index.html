Lesson 7

We Can Fix It, Can't We?

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
On Friday evening the internet in our flat stopped working. For my family it was a disaster. Here is the full record of the conversation. Mum: Somebody should restart the router, shouldn't they? Dad: I have done it twice. It can't be the router, can it? My sister Liza: I must send my project tonight. You can fix it, can't you, Dad? Dad: I am an accountant, not a magician. We could call the company, couldn't we? Mum: They won't answer at nine in the evening, will they? Me: We should check the cable first, shouldn't we? Everybody looked at me. Dad: You couldn't do it yourself, could you? Me: I can try. I followed the cable from the router along the wall, behind the sofa and under the carpet. Near the balcony door I found the problem. Our cat, Marquis, was sitting next to a cable that had been chewed in two. Me: Cats mustn't eat cables, must they? Marquis didn't answer. Liza: He will be OK, won't he? Mum: He looks fine. He should see a vet anyway, shouldn't he? Dad: We can't punish him, can we? He doesn't understand. Marquis looked at us as if we were the problem. Dad went to the shop for a new cable. Liza sent her project from my phone. And for one evening, without the internet, we played a board game together. All four of us. And the cat. Mum: We should do this more often, shouldn't we? Nobody argued. But the next morning the first thing everybody did was to check the wi-fi.
