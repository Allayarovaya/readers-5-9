Lesson 6

A Train Across the Map

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
Last summer my dad and I travelled by train from Uzhhorod to Kharkiv. It took almost a whole day. I sat by the window, and the map of Ukraine from my Geography textbook came alive. We left Uzhhorod in the evening. The train climbed slowly through the Carpathians. I saw dark forests, wooden houses and a river that ran next to the railway. There were tunnels, and my ears felt strange. In one village children waved to the train. I waved back. I woke up near Lviv. The mountains had gone. Now there were low green hills, small fields and churches with silver roofs. After Ternopil the land became flat. The fields got bigger and bigger. Wheat, corn, sunflowers, then wheat again. Storks stood in the meadows. Villages with white houses and cherry gardens dotted the plain. At noon we crossed the Dnipro near Kyiv. I had seen it in photos, but the real river was much wider. The train was on the bridge for a whole minute. On the high right bank the golden domes were shining. After Kyiv we travelled across the Left Bank. Poltava region looked like a painting: groves, ponds, and fields to the horizon. Dad told me that some of the most fertile soil in the country is here. In the evening the landscape changed again. There were more factories and bigger towns. We arrived in Kharkiv at night. The station was huge and full of light. "One country," said Dad. "And how many different lands!" I didn't sleep much on that train. I didn't want to miss anything.
