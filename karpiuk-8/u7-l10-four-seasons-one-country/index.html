Lesson 10

Four Seasons, One Country

Listen & Read
Listen to the text
Speed:
My penfriend from Kenya once asked me, "What is the weather like in Ukraine?" I started to write and understood that there is no short answer. The climate of Ukraine is moderate and continental. This means that we have four real seasons, and they are very different. The climate is determined by the country's position: it is far from the ocean, and there are no high mountains in the north to stop cold air. Winters are cold. The average temperature in January ranges from about minus seven degrees in the north-east to plus two or three on the southern coast. Snow can lie for three months in the Carpathians and for only a few days near the sea. Summers are warm and sometimes hot. In July the average temperature varies from about eighteen degrees in the west to twenty-three or more in the south. A summer day of thirty-five degrees doesn't surprise anybody. The amount of rain varies too. The Carpathians are the wettest region. The steppe in the south is the driest, and long periods without rain can cause problems for farmers. The seas have a strong influence on the weather near the coast. Winters there are milder, and autumn is long and warm. Spring comes from the south-west and moves to the north-east. So when cherry trees are already in blossom in Uzhhorod, people in Sumy are still wearing coats. I wrote all this to my penfriend. He answered, "Four seasons? Snow? Twenty degrees of difference in one country? You are lucky. Here it is always the same." I had never thought about it that way.
